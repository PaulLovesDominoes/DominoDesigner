# Domino Inventory

The inventory is the list of domino colors you own. Every color you can paint with in
[Editing Dominoes](domino-editing), and every color an [image mapping](image-mapping) is allowed to
choose from, comes from this list.

A fresh inventory starts with eleven common colors so there is something to build with right away.
Change them, delete them, or replace the whole list with your own.

## The table

Each row is one kind of domino you own.

- **Active** — whether this color is offered when you paint. See below.
- **Available** — how many you have
- **Color Name** — what you call it
- **Color** — the swatch itself; click it to open the color picker
- **Material / Finish / Brand** — what the dominoes are
    -  Note:  There may be changes to these columns in the future, specifically, "Brand" may be replaced with "Dimensions" - because that's what really matters to this program.
- **Shortcut** — up to 3 characters for picking this color from the keyboard. See below.
- **Notes** — anything you want to remember about them

Click any cell to edit it. Changes take effect immediately.

Click a column heading to sort by it, and click the same heading again to reverse the order.
Sorting by **Color** walks the color wheel — blacks, whites and greys first, then the colors in
rainbow order — which is usually a better way to find a color than its name.

## Active and inactive colors

Only **active** colors appear as swatches when you edit dominoes, and only active colors are used
when mapping an image.

An inactive row stays in the list, greyed out, and can be switched back on at any time. This is for
colors you own but do not want in the build you are working on — switch them off rather than
deleting them, and the rest of your list is unaffected.

## Shortcuts

While editing domino colors you can type a color's shortcut instead of hunting for its swatch. A
shortcut is up to 3 letters or digits, and no two colors should share one — if they do, neither can
be reached by typing.

Colors that arrive from an uploaded file without a shortcut are given a unique one automatically.

## Adding and deleting colors

Use **New** to add a color. It arrives at the top of the list, in grey, called "New Color", and
stays at the top however you sort until you rename it — so a new row never hides from you.

To delete, tick the checkbox on each row you want gone and press the trash-can button. **Deleting
cannot be undone.**

## Changing a color changes your build

The dominoes on your build plane refer to this list rather than keeping their own copy of a color,
so:

- **Editing a color** — recoloring "Red" to a different red — immediately recolors every domino
  already painted with it, anywhere in the build.
- **Deleting a color** leaves any domino painted with it showing the plain default grey.

This is usually what you want: it means you can adjust a color late in a design and see the whole
build follow.

## Upload and download

A list of a few hundred colors is easier to maintain in your own spreadsheet, so the inventory can be saved to
a file and read back.

- **Download CSV** writes the list in the order you are looking at it.
- **Upload CSV** **replaces the whole Domino Designer inventory.** Before anything changes, a dialog tells you what the file would do — how many colors are arriving, how many keep their identity, and anything it had to skip or repair. Nothing happens until you agree, and it cannot be undone afterwards.

A color that keeps its identity keeps its dominoes: they stay painted. A file whose colors are all
new to this build leaves every painted domino without its color, and the dialog says so before you
commit.

You do not have to write the file yourself from scratch. Only the name and the count are required,
column headings are matched loosely, and a row with no color value at all comes in grey and
switched off, ready for you to pick its color in the app.

## Counts are not enforced

Nothing stops you designing a build that needs more dominoes than you own. **Available** is there
for your own planning, and the legend on a printed [build plan](build-plans) tells you how many of
each color the design actually needs.

NOTE:  This is one of the things which will likely change in the future.
